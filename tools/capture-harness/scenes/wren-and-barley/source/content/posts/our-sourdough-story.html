People ask what makes our sourdough different, and the honest answer is time. The dough rests for a full day before it sees the oven, and most of that day it is just sitting in a cool room doing nothing we can see.

We mix at four in the morning with flour, water, salt and the starter. By seven the dough has been folded three times. It is shaped around midday and goes into the fridge overnight, which is where the flavour and the open crumb come from.

The next morning the loaves are scored and baked at a high heat with steam for the first twenty minutes, then dry for the rest. That is the whole recipe. The rest is paying attention.
